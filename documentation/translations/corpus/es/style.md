Spanish, as written in Spain, the way one Home Assistant user explains something to another.

## Address

- Informal, always: "tú", "tu", "tus", and the verb in the second person singular. Never "usted". Home Assistant's Spanish interface says "tú" too ("te permite", "puedes"), and so do Spook's own Spanish strings.
- Instructions in the imperative singular: "Ve a **Configuración**", "Selecciona **Guardar**".
- "We" stays "nosotros", mostly in the verb only: "vemos", "hacemos". Frenck speaks as "yo" where the English has "I" or "my", again mostly in the verb.
- Leave out the subject pronoun where Spanish leaves it out: "Puedes", not "Tú puedes", unless it is emphasis.

## Tone

- Short sentences. One thought each. Spanish allows long chains with "el cual" and gerunds: split them.
- No bureaucratic Spanish: "proceder a", "llevar a cabo", "en lo que respecta a", "cabe destacar", "a efectos de". Write the plain verb.
- No gerund chains: "Abre la automatización y selecciona...", not "Abriendo la automatización, seleccionando...".
- Dry humor stays dry. "That is a bit late." is "Eso es un poco tarde.", not a joke with a wink. No exclamation marks the English does not have.

## Words

- "Select" is "selecciona". Never "haz clic" or "pulsa".
- Use the words Home Assistant uses in Spanish: automatización, entidad, dispositivo, área, planta, disparador, condición, acción, integración, estado. The glossary has the list.
- Home Assistant's Spanish interface translates more than other languages do: a dashboard is a "panel de control", a blueprint is a "plano", a template is a "plantilla", a helper is an "ayudante", a floor is a "planta". Follow the interface.
- "Bug" is "fallo" in running text, or "error" when that is what it is.
- "Spook" is masculine and takes no article: "Spook avisa", "Spook está instalado". Never "el Spook", and never "el fantasma" for the product.
- "Home Assistant" takes no article: "Home Assistant se reinicia", "la versión de Home Assistant".
- No em-dashes and no en-dashes. Use a full stop, a colon, a comma or brackets.

## Typography

- Questions and exclamations open and close: "¿No hay nada?", "¡Listo!". The opening mark goes where the question starts, which can be in the middle of a sentence: "Si no hay nada, ¿qué pasa?".
- Quotes: "straight double quotes", like the interface. Never inside a component attribute like `alt`, where no quote of any kind is needed.
- No space before `:`, `;`, `?` or `!`.
- A sentence after a colon starts lowercase, unless it is a name.
- Capitals only at the start of a sentence and for names. Not in every word of a title: "Actualizar Spook", not "Actualizar Spook En Home Assistant".
- Numbers and versions as in the English. Times in `code` are not touched.

## Wording that repeats

Sentences that come back on many pages, written one way. Chosen for consistency on 2026-11-25 by an agent, not by a native speaker: three translators worked in parallel, and the wording drifted. Each is the variant most pages used, unless the note says otherwise. Not yet read by a native speaker.

- "How to use it" follows the gender of what it is about: "Cómo usarla" for an action or a condition, "Cómo usarlo" for a trigger or an entity.
- "Just want to see it work?" is "¿Solo quieres verla funcionar?".
- "New to repairs?" is "¿Nuevo con las reparaciones?", and "New to this?" is "¿Nuevo con esto?": "con", not "en", so the two match.
- "You find them wherever you add an action" is "Las encuentras en cualquier sitio donde añades una acción".
- "Let us know in our discussion forums" is "Avísanos en nuestros foros de discusión".
- "Let us know, and we'll add it to the list!" is "¡Avísanos y lo añadimos a la lista!". The English has the exclamation mark, so the Spanish opens and closes one.
- "Made one, or found one?" is "¿Has hecho uno, o has encontrado uno?".
- "Got an idea to take this further" is "¿Tienes una idea para ir más allá?".
- "Stuck, or found a bug?" is "¿Atascado, o has encontrado un fallo?".
- "Tells you where to go for help" is "te dice adónde ir para pedir ayuda".
- "Nearly certain what you meant" is "casi seguro de lo que querías decir".
- "Add one directly to your own instance" is "Añade uno directamente a tu instancia".
- "More than one entity at once, as a list" is "más de una entidad a la vez, como una lista", and "write them as a list" is "escríbelos como una lista".
- "Anything you leave out keeps the value it already has" is "Lo que dejes fuera conserva el valor que ya tiene". There were three variants; "dejes fuera" and "conserva" were each the majority half.
- "Fields you leave off keep their value" is "Los campos que dejes desactivados conservan su valor".
- "You need to give at least one thing to change" is "Tienes que dar al menos una cosa que cambiar".
- "Spook looks again when you save" is "Spook vuelve a mirar cuando guardas".
- "Everything in the list is checked first" is "Primero se comprueba todo lo que hay en la lista".
- "Asking for one is an error" is "Pedir una es un error".
- "Open the automation or script that should do the deleting" is "Abre la automatización o el script que debe hacer la eliminación".
- "Made by hand, that helper stays on the helpers page long after" is "Hecho a mano, ese ayudante se queda en la página de ayudantes mucho después": a tie, and the shorter one is closer to "long after". "Made with this action" is "hecho con esta acción".
- "This is one check, shared by every helper" is "Es una sola comprobación, compartida por todos los ayudantes".
- "An attribute is an extra detail" is "Un atributo es un dato extra".
- "Automations and scripts are not affected" is "A las automatizaciones y scripts no les afecta".
- "They change your setup" is "Cambian tu configuración".
- "This action does all of that" is "Esta acción lo hace todo".
- "An icon that was renamed or removed" is "un icono al que se le cambió el nombre o que se quitó".
- "A frontend run from a development build" is "un frontend que se ejecuta desde una versión de desarrollo".
