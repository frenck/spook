German, as written in Germany, the way one Home Assistant user explains something to another.

## Address

- Informal, always: "du", "dein", lowercase. Never "Sie" or "Ihr". Home Assistant's own German interface says "du" too, so the docs and the product sound like one thing.
- Instructions in the imperative singular: "Gehe zu **Einstellungen**", "Wähle **Speichern** aus".
- "We" stays "wir". Frenck speaks as "ich" where the English has "I" or "my".

## Tone

- Short main clauses. German invites long nested sentences: do not accept the invitation. Two short sentences beat one with three commas.
- No officialese: "erfolgt", "mittels", "bezüglich", "diesbezüglich", "gegebenenfalls", "zwecks". Use a verb and say who does what.
- No noun chains where a verb works: "um die Automation zu reparieren", not "zur Durchführung der Reparatur der Automation".
- Dry humor stays dry, and German dry is drier. No "leider", no "einfach" and no "ganz einfach" the English does not have. No exclamation marks the English does not have.

## Words

- "Select" is "wähle ... aus". Never "klicke".
- Use the words Home Assistant uses in German: Automation, Entität, Gerät, Bereich, Etage, Auslöser, Bedingung, Aktion, Integration, Zustand. The glossary has the list.
- Home Assistant's interface says "Automation" and "Automationen", not "Automatisierung". Follow the interface.
- English words German Home Assistant users use stay English: Dashboard, Blueprint, Template, Label.
- Compounds with a name or an English part get a hyphen: "Spook-Integration", "YAML-Konfiguration", "Debug-Protokoll".
- "Home Assistant" is never hyphenated into a compound. Not "Home-Assistant-Version" but "Version von Home Assistant", not "Home-Assistant-Projekt" but "das Projekt Home Assistant". The name stays as it is on the screen.
- "Spook" stays "Spook". It is a name, not "der Spuk" and not "das Gespenst".

## Typography

- Quotes: „unten und oben“, the German way. Never inside a component attribute like `alt`, where no double quote of any kind is needed.
- Nouns are capitalized, also English ones in a German sentence: "das Dashboard", "der Trigger".
- A full sentence after a colon starts with a capital. A fragment does not.
- Numbers and versions as in the English. Times in `code` are not touched.
