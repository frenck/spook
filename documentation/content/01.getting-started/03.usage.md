You [installed Spook](/installation). Now what? This tour takes about ten minutes and shows you the three places where Spook shows up. There is nothing new to learn: Spook lives inside the :term[Home Assistant] screens you already use.

## 1. See what Spook found {#repairs}

From the moment it is installed, Spook looks through your automations, scripts, dashboards and more for things that no longer exist, like the motion sensor you replaced last month. What it finds, it reports in the :term[repairs dashboard]{to="repairs"}.

1. In Home Assistant, go to **Settings**.
2. Select **Repairs**. Or use this button to jump straight there:

   [![Open your Home Assistant instance and show your repairs.](https://my.home-assistant.io/badges/repairs.svg)](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/repairs/)

3. Look for issues that mention Spook, and select one to read it.

::screenshot{name="repairs-list-movie-night" alt="The repairs dashboard with Spook's repairs, each saying by Spook under its name."}
Spook's repairs say "by Spook" under their name.

::

::screenshot{name="repair-unknown-entities" alt="A repair raised by Spook, listing the entities an automation uses that no longer exist."}
This automation uses some entities that do not exist.

::

Every repair from Spook tells you what it found, where, and how to fix it. Some come with a button that fixes it for you.

Nothing there? Then your setup is clean, or Spook is still looking. A few checks wait ten minutes after Home Assistant starts, so they don't slow it down.

Found one? The guide [Fix your first repair](/fix-your-first-repair) walks you through it.

## 2. Try your first action {#actions}

:term[Actions]{to="action"} are the "do something" steps of your :term[automations]{to="automation"} and :term[scripts]{to="script"}. Spook adds over a hundred new ones, and marks every one of them with a 👻.

Try one that is harmless and makes its point: snoozing an automation.

1. Go to **Settings** > **Automations & scenes** and open any automation.
2. Select **Add action**.
3. Search for **snooze**, and select **Automation: Snooze 👻**.
4. Choose which automation to snooze, and for how long.

::screenshot{name="action-snooze" alt="The Add action dialog, searched for snooze, with Automation: Snooze 👻 outlined."}
Search for snooze, and there it is. Every action Spook added carries a 👻.

::

You don't have to save it. The point is where to look: whenever you add an action, search for what you want to do, and the ones with a 👻 are Spook's.

Curious what else there is? The [actions list](/actions) has all of them on one page.

## 3. Meet the new entities {#devices-entities}

Spook also adds :term[devices]{to="device"} and :term[entities]{to="entity"}: switches, sensors and buttons for parts of Home Assistant that had none. You use them like any other, in dashboards and automations.

1. Go to **Settings** > **Devices & services**.
2. Select **Spook**. Or use this button:

   [![Open your Home Assistant instance and show an integration.](https://my.home-assistant.io/badges/integration.svg)](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/integration/?domain=spook)

3. Select one of the devices to see what it offers.

::screenshot{name="spook-integration" alt="The Spook integration page, with its devices for Home Assistant Link, Home Assistant and Repairs."}
The Spook integration page, with the devices it added.

::
