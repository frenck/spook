Spook reported a problem, and you want it gone. This guide uses the most common one as its example: an automation that uses an :term[entity] that no longer exists. Every other repair from Spook works the same way.

**You need:** Spook [installed](/installation), and a repair from Spook in your :term[repairs dashboard]{to="repairs"}. It takes about five minutes.

## What happened?

At some point you renamed, replaced or removed something: a lamp, a motion sensor, a whole integration. An automation that used it still points at the old name. Home Assistant runs the automation without complaining, and the step that uses the missing thing does nothing.

Spook noticed, and raised a repair so you know.

## Step 1: Open the repair

1. In :term[Home Assistant], go to **Settings** > **Repairs**. Or use this button:

   [![Open your Home Assistant instance and show your repairs.](https://my.home-assistant.io/badges/repairs.svg)](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/repairs/)

2. Select the issue that starts with **Unknown entities used in**.

::screenshot{name="repairs-list-movie-night" alt="The repairs dashboard with Spook's repairs, the one about unknown entities used in Movie night ringed."}
The repairs dashboard. Spook's repairs say "by Spook" under their name.

::

::screenshot{name="repair-unknown-entities" alt="A repair raised by Spook, naming the automation and listing the entities it cannot find."}
The repair names the automation, and lists what it cannot find.

::

## Step 2: Read what Spook found

The repair tells you two things:

- **Which automation** has the problem. Its name is a link.
- **Which entities** are missing, as a list of :term[entity IDs]{to="entity id"} like `light.living_room_lamp`. An entity ID is the unique name Home Assistant uses for an entity behind the scenes.

Take a moment to recognize the names. Usually it is something you renamed or replaced, and you know right away what it should be now.

## Step 3: Fix the automation

1. In the repair, select the name of the automation. It opens in the automation editor.
2. Look through the triggers, conditions and actions for the missing entity. The repair lists its entity ID, so you know what to look for.

::screenshot{name="automation-movie-night" alt="The Movie night automation in the editor, where the TV it waits for and the two lamps it turns off each show as Unknown entity."}
What is missing shows as **Unknown entity**, where a working one shows its name.

::

3. Do one of two things:
   - **It was renamed or replaced:** select the entity, and pick the right one from the list.
   - **It is gone for good:** remove the step that uses it.
4. Select **Save**.

## Step 4: Watch the repair disappear

Go back to **Settings** > **Repairs**. The issue is gone.

You don't have to mark anything as done. Spook looks at the automation again the moment you save it, and takes the repair away by itself once nothing is missing.

Still there? Then the automation uses the missing entity in more than one place. Open the repair again: the list now shows what is left.

:star-ask

## When Spook is wrong, or you want to keep it this way

Sometimes a missing entity is fine. A device that is only plugged in around the holidays, for example.

- **Ignore the repair.** Open it and select **Ignore**. Spook remembers, and won't raise it again for the same findings.
- **Turn the automation off.** Spook leaves automations alone that are turned off, and looks again when you turn one back on.

Think Spook got it wrong? [Let us know](/support). A repair that cries wolf is a bug.

## What's next?

Other repairs follow the same four steps: open it, read it, fix it where it points, and it disappears by itself. The page of each integration explains every repair Spook can raise for it, such as [all repairs for automations](/automation#repairs).
