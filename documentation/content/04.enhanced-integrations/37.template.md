The [Template integration](https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/template/) lets you make your own entities, whose state and actions come from :term[templates]{to="template"}. A template is a small piece of code that works out a value from other entities. Like a sensor that adds up three others, or a switch that runs a script.

A template gets nothing back when the entity it reads is gone, and no error tells you. The same goes for an attribute it reads: an extra detail on an entity, like the brightness of a light. An action that no longer exists fails when it runs. Spook reads your templates and raises a :term[repair]{to="repairs"} when they point at something Home Assistant does not have.

That goes for template helpers made in the UI, and for template entities set up in YAML, under `template:` in your configuration, trigger based ones included.

## Repairs

Spook looks through your template helpers and template entities for things that can no longer work, and tells you in the :term[repairs dashboard]{to="repairs"}. Each repair names the helper or template entity and what is wrong with it.

Fixing a template helper goes the same way each time: open the helper from the repair, change its template, and save. For a template entity from YAML there is no editor: edit it under `template:` in your configuration, and reload your template entities. Spook looks again, and takes the repair away by itself. New to this? Follow [Fix your first repair](/fix-your-first-repair).

### Unknown referenced entities

A template uses an entity that no longer exists, usually because it was renamed, replaced or removed. The template gets nothing back for it, and a trigger on it never fires.

**How to fix it:** Open the helper from the repair, and in its template, use the right entity. Or take it out. For a template entity from YAML, the repair names it and its entity ID: change it under `template:` in your configuration, and reload your template entities. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

Spook looks in the templates of Template helpers for entity IDs that do not exist. The repair lists the helper and the missing entity IDs.

Template entities from YAML are checked the same way: every template they have, like the state, availability, attributes, icon and picture, their variables, and their own actions, like the `turn_on` of a template switch. For a trigger based template entity, the triggers, conditions, actions and variables of its section are checked as well. A trigger on an entity that does not exist never fires, so the template entity never updates.

Each template entity gets a repair of its own. When the triggers of a section name an entity that does not exist, every template entity in that section gets a repair, since none of them updates. What only a disabled trigger or step names is listed as such. `this` and `trigger` are only known while running, so whatever they point at is not checked.

An entity spelled out after `states`, like `states.light.kitchen.state`, is not checked when that `states` is not Home Assistant's: when a template defines its own with a macro or `{% set %}`, or a template entity has a variable of that name. Used as a filter, like `'light.kitchen' | states`, it still is Home Assistant's, and still checked. An entity ID in quotes is checked wherever it is, whatever it is handed to.

:::
::

### Unknown referenced actions

A template helper or template entity runs an action that does not exist, usually because the integration or script that provided it was removed. Like the `press` of a template button, or the `turn_on` of a template switch. That action fails when it runs.

**How to fix it:** Open the helper from the repair, and change the action to one that exists. Or bring back the integration or script it came from. For a template entity from YAML, change it under `template:` in your configuration, and reload your template entities. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

Spook looks at the action sequences of Template helpers, such as the `press` action of a Template Button or the `turn_on` and `turn_off` actions of a Template Switch. When an action does not exist, the repair lists the helper and the missing action.

Template entities from YAML are checked the same way: every action of their own, like the `turn_on` and `turn_off` of a template switch, the `press` of a template button or the `set_value` of a template number, and for a trigger based template entity the actions of its section. Each template entity gets a repair of its own, naming the template entity and its entity ID.

Templated action names are not checked, because what they end up as is only known while running. Actions of an integration you disabled are not reported either: switched off on purpose is not gone. Once you enable the integration again, they are checked as usual.

:::
::

### Unknown referenced attributes

A template reads an attribute its entity does not have, like `state_attr('light.kitchen', 'brightnes')`. It gets nothing back, and you get no error.

**How to fix it:** Open the helper from the repair, and use an attribute the entity has. When Spook is nearly certain what you meant, the repair says so. For a template entity from YAML, change it under `template:` in your configuration, and reload your template entities. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

Spook looks at the attributes Template helpers and template entities from YAML use: attributes named in their templates, like `state_attr('light.kitchen', 'brightness')`, and the `attribute` of state and numeric state conditions and triggers in their actions. For a trigger based template entity from YAML, that includes the triggers and conditions of its section. When a helper or template entity uses an attribute its entity does not have, Spook raises a repair. It lists the helper, the attribute and the entity, and what was most likely meant when Spook is near certain of it.

A template reading an attribute that never shows up gets nothing back. You get no error.

Many attributes only show up some of the time: a media player that is off drops most of its own, and integrations add their own that come and go. So an attribute is only reported when Spook cannot find it anywhere: not on the entity right now, not as something that kind of entity offers in Home Assistant, and not in anything the recorder remembers the entity having. An entity the recorder does not record is only checked for attributes that differ from a real one in upper and lower case alone, like `Brightness`. An attribute or entity that is only worked out while running, from a variable for example, is not checked.

Spook does not look right after Home Assistant starts, when the recorder is busy, but ten minutes later. After that it looks again when a Template helper is added, changed or removed, when template entities are reloaded, when an entity is added or removed, when the entity registry changes, when an integration loads, and once a day.

:::
::
