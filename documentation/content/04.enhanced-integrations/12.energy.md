The energy :term[dashboard] in :term[Home Assistant] is where your consumption, production, gas, water, and battery numbers come together. It is configured once, in **Settings** > **Dashboards** > **Energy**, by pointing it at the :term[entities]{to="entity"} that carry those numbers.

That configuration is a list of :term[entity IDs]{to="entity id"}, kept separately from the entities themselves. Home Assistant does validate it, but only shows the result inside that configuration panel. You visit that page when you change something, not to check on it.

Spook takes Home Assistant's own check and puts what it finds in your :term[repairs] dashboard.

## Repairs

Spook checks your energy configuration for entities that no longer exist, and tells you in the :term[repairs dashboard]{to="repairs"}. New to repairs? Follow [Fix your first repair](/fix-your-first-repair).

### Unknown referenced entities

The energy dashboard uses an entity that no longer exists, usually because it was removed or replaced, or did not load. The dashboard does not complain: it leaves that source out, so a missing gas sensor looks like a month where you used no gas.

::screenshot{name="repair-energy-unknown-references" alt="The repair for an energy dashboard that uses an entity that is gone, with the choice to take it out."}
Spook can take what is gone out of the energy configuration for you.

::

**How to fix it:** Select the repair. It gives you three choices:

- **Take the deleted ones out of the energy configuration**, which removes those the repair marks as deleted, ones Home Assistant removed, the way each setting allows. A missing meter takes its source or device along: a solar panel, a battery, a gas or water meter, or one way of a grid connection or of a battery measured per direction. A missing price or cost is only cleared, so the meter it belonged to keeps counting. A device that was part of a device that goes stops saying so.
- **Let me do it myself**, which points you at **Settings** > **Dashboards** > **Energy**.
- **Keep them, stop telling me**, which leaves them alone and stops the repair coming back.

Was the entity renamed and not removed, like a device that was paired again? Then pick the new one in the energy configuration yourself. Taking it out means the dashboard stops showing the history of that source; the statistics themselves stay. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

Spook runs Home Assistant's own energy validation and looks for one result out of the several it can return: an entity with no state at all. That is usually one that was removed. The same result also covers an entity that never loaded or failed to set up, so treat it as something to check, not as a verdict. That is also why Spook only takes out the ones Home Assistant deleted. A button that removes something needs proof the entity was removed, and Spook only takes Home Assistant's own word for that: the entity registry keeps a record of every entity it deleted, for a month after nothing owns it any more. Something that is merely not there yet looks exactly like something that is gone: a YAML sensor without a unique ID whose integration is still starting, or an entity of an integration that is retrying its setup. Neither has a state, neither is in the registry, and Spook cannot tell them apart. So those are reported, and left for you to check. The other results are either temporary (an entity that happens to be unavailable right now) or a configuration choice, so Spook leaves those alone.

The dashboard draws the sources it can still read and leaves out the one it cannot. The graph still looks plausible, and is wrong.

Spook looks again before it takes anything out, and only touches what the list you read and that fresh look agree on. An entity that is back by the time you choose is left where it is. If none of them is still unknown, Spook says so.

:::
::

## Use cases

What people use this for:

- Replacing an energy meter. The new integration gives you new entity IDs, the energy dashboard keeps the old ones, and the totals stop adding up.
- Renaming a sensor that feeds the dashboard. Renaming is quick, and it is easy to forget that the energy configuration still points at the old name.
