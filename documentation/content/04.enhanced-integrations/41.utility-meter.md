The utility meter :term[helper] tracks how much of something you use: electricity, gas, water. It follows a source sensor, like the reading of your smart meter, and counts the usage per day, month, or whatever cycle you pick.

A utility meter only works as long as the sensor it meters exists. Spook tells you when it does not.

## Repairs

Spook looks through your utility meters for a source sensor that no longer exists, and tells you in the :term[repairs dashboard]{to="repairs"}. New to repairs? Follow [Fix your first repair](/fix-your-first-repair).

### Unknown source entity

A utility meter meters a sensor that no longer exists, usually because it was removed or replaced. The meter has nothing to count, so it stops counting.

**How to fix it:** Open the repair. Spook offers to remove the utility meter for you, once Home Assistant deleted the sensor it is missing. One that may just not be there yet, like one of an integration that is still starting up, gets no button. To keep it and its history, pick **Let me fix it myself**: open the utility meter on the **Helpers** page, under **Settings** > **Devices & services**, and pick the right sensor under **Input sensor** in its options. Or bring back the sensor that went missing. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

Spook reads the configuration of every utility meter made in the UI, and raises a repair when the source sensor it meters is unknown to Home Assistant. The repair names the utility meter and the missing source entity.

Spook reads the configuration, not the running entity. So a utility meter that failed to set up entirely is still checked, and that is the one most likely to be broken. Utility meters set up in YAML are not checked.

This is one check, shared by every helper built on another entity. The [Home Assistant page](/homeassistant#unknown-helper-sources) explains it in full.
