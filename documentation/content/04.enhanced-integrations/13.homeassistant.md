`homeassistant` is the :term[integration] that is :term[Home Assistant] itself. It keeps the lists that everything else hangs off, which Home Assistant calls registries: the :term[areas]{to="area"}, :term[floors]{to="floor"}, :term[labels]{to="label"}, :term[devices]{to="device"} and :term[entities]{to="entity"} that give your configuration its shape, plus the `homeassistant:` section of your :term[YAML].

An entry in those lists stays until something removes it, and almost nothing ever does. That is the right default. It is also why a Home Assistant that has been running for a few years collects things that refer to nothing: an area nobody uses, a label on nothing, a helper built on a sensor you threw out.

Spook finds that layer for you, and offers to clean it up.

## Devices & entities

Spook turns Home Assistant itself into a device, with buttons to reload and restart it and a set of counting sensors: your entities in total, one per entity type for a long list of types, and counts of things like areas, devices and integrations. Those are documented on [Devices & entities](/devices-entities).

## Repairs

Spook looks through Home Assistant itself for things that point at nothing, or do nothing, and tells you in the :term[repairs dashboard]{to="repairs"}. New to repairs? Follow [Fix your first repair](/fix-your-first-repair).

Some of these are broken things, and some are only untidy. The untidy ones can all be fixed from the repair itself. Each of them also offers to stop mentioning it: an empty area you keep on purpose is your business.

The tidiness repairs also hold off for a day, so setting something up and filling it in after lunch does not earn you a repair in between. A new area, floor or label is left alone for its first twenty-four hours. A blueprint is judged by when its file was last touched, not by when it first appeared. That also spares one you edited yesterday.

### Unknown area sensors

An :term[area] uses a temperature or humidity sensor that no longer exists. Assist cannot answer "how warm is it in the kitchen?" anymore, and area cards lose their reading.

**How to fix it:** Select the repair. **Clear the setting** takes the missing sensor off the area, so you can pick a new one. **Let me fix it myself** lets you pick another sensor in the settings of the area. **Keep it, stop telling me** leaves it as it is. If the sensor was renamed, the repair suggests the entity it most likely became. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

An :term[area] can have a temperature and a humidity sensor of its own. That is where Assist gets the answer to "how warm is it in the kitchen?", and where area cards get their reading.

Home Assistant only checks that sensor when you pick it. Remove it, or rename its entity ID, and the area keeps pointing at something that is not there anymore. Spook checks each area's sensors against the entities Home Assistant knows, once it has started, so a sensor that is still loading is not mistaken for a missing one. If the sensor was renamed, the repair suggests the entity it most likely became.

Clearing only takes away a setting that points at nothing, so nothing that worked stops working.

:::
::

### Unknown customized entities

The `customize:` section of your configuration names an entity that does not exist. That block of YAML does nothing at all.

**How to fix it:** Remove those entities from the `customize:` section of your configuration, and restart Home Assistant. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

The `customize:` section of your configuration pins attributes onto a specific entity. That is how a sensor gets a friendly name or a different icon that its integration does not offer.

Spook checks the entity each customization names. If the entity does not exist, that block of YAML does nothing at all. Only exact entity keys are checked. Glob and domain customizations are patterns, not references, so they are left alone.

:::
::

### Unknown helper sources

A :term[helper] is built on an entity that no longer exists: a threshold on a sensor that is gone, a utility meter on an energy reading that was replaced. Without its source, the helper cannot work.

**How to fix it:** Select the repair. **Remove this helper** deletes the helper for you. **Let me fix it myself** sends you to the helpers page, to point it at another entity. **Keep it, stop telling me** leaves it as it is. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

A number of :term[helpers]{to="helper"} are built on top of another entity: a threshold on a sensor, a derivative on a counter, a utility meter on an energy reading. Spook knows which thirteen helper types store a source reference, and where each one keeps it. It reads that configuration, and raises a repair when a source is unknown to Home Assistant.

Groups and min/max helpers are handled elsewhere on purpose. Both have a repair of their own that can do more than report the problem.

Spook reads the configuration, not the running helper. So a helper whose entity failed to set up entirely is still checked, and that is the helper most likely to be broken.

:::
::

### Unknown min/max helper members

A min/max helper combines entities that no longer exist. It carries on with the ones it has left, so the number it shows is based on less than you think.

**How to fix it:** Select the repair. **Remove these members** drops the missing ones, and the helper carries on with the rest. **Let me fix it myself** sends you to the helpers page. **Keep them, stop telling me** leaves them as they are. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

A min/max helper combines several entities into one number. Spook reads its configuration and raises a repair when it names members that no longer exist.

A min/max helper needs at least two members, though. If removing the missing ones would leave fewer than that, Spook says so and sends you to the Helpers page instead.

:::
::

### Unknown icons

Something in Home Assistant has an icon that does not exist, usually because of a typo or a renamed icon: an entity, a helper, a zone, an area, a floor, a label or a category. It shows an empty spot where the icon should be.

::screenshot{name="repair-unknown-icons" alt="The Unknown icons repair, listing a misspelled icon on a helper with the icon Spook thinks was meant."}
Nearly certain what you meant? The repair says so.

::

**How to fix it:** The repair lists each icon and where it was set. Give each of them an icon that exists, in that place. When Spook is nearly certain what you meant, the repair says so. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

The icons you set in Home Assistant itself are checked against the Material Design Icons that come with it: the icon you gave an :term[entity] in its settings, the icon of a :term[helper] or a zone, of an :term[area], :term[floor], :term[label] or category, and the icons in `customize:` and `customize_domain:`. An `mdi:` icon that does not exist is drawn as an empty spot, without an error. Spook raises one repair listing each of these with where it was set, and suggests the icon you most likely meant when it is nearly certain.

This usually happens with a typo, an icon that was renamed or removed, or one picked from the Material Design Icons website that is newer than the version Home Assistant comes with. The repair says which version that is.

:::

:::accordion-item{label="Known limitations" icon="i-mdi-alert-outline"}

- Only icons starting with `mdi:` are checked. The older `hass:` prefix, and icons from sets you added, like those from the Marketplace, are left alone.
- The icon an integration gives its own entities is not checked. That is the integration's to fix, not yours.
- `customize_glob:` is not checked. Home Assistant only keeps the patterns it turns those into, which do not say which part of your configuration they came from.
- The list of icons is read from the frontend Home Assistant has installed. A frontend run from a development build is not taken into account, and a frontend without that list means nothing is checked.

:::
::

### Empty areas

An :term[area] has no devices, no entities, and nothing uses it. It does nothing, except take up room in every area picker.

::screenshot{name="repair-fix-empty-area" alt="The Empty area repair for the attic, with its three choices: remove it, let me fix it, or keep it."}
Spook offers to remove the area, or to leave it to you.

::

**How to fix it:** Select the repair. **Remove this empty area** deletes it. **Let me fix it myself** leaves the choice to you. **Keep it, stop telling me** is for an area you keep on purpose, like the attic you have not got around to. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

An :term[area] with no devices, no entities, no vacuum cleaning it, and no mention anywhere in an automation or script is not doing anything.

Spook checks references as well as contents, so an area that is deliberately empty because something uses it is left alone. Being mentioned is enough: it does not have to be _targeted_. An area listed in a `repeat` block's `for_each` is the target of nothing, and Home Assistant does not report it as a reference, but a script plainly needs it. This repair offers to delete the area, so anything naming it is reason enough to leave it be.

An area a vacuum has its rooms mapped to is left alone too. Nothing may live there, but cleaning it by name, from an action or by voice, needs it to exist.

:::
::

### Empty floors

A :term[floor] has no areas, and nothing uses it.

**How to fix it:** Select the repair. **Remove this empty floor** deletes it. **Let me fix it myself** leaves the choice to you, like adding areas to it. **Keep it, stop telling me** keeps it and stops the repair. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

A :term[floor] exists to group areas. One with no areas at all, and no mention anywhere in an automation or script, is not doing that.

As with empty areas, being mentioned is enough. It does not have to be targeted, because this repair offers to delete the floor.

:::
::

### Unused labels

A :term[label] is on no entity, device or area, and nothing uses it.

**How to fix it:** Select the repair. **Remove this unused label** deletes it. **Let me fix it myself** leaves the choice to you. **Keep it, stop telling me** keeps it and stops the repair. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

A :term[label] that is applied to no entity, device or area, and that nothing anywhere in an automation or script mentions, is not doing anything either.

As with empty areas, being mentioned is enough. It does not have to be targeted, because this repair offers to delete the label.

:::
::

## Use cases

What people use this for:

- Cleaning up after a reorganisation. Renaming and regrouping leaves empty areas, empty floors and unused labels behind. You only see them when you go looking.
- Finding helpers that broke months ago. Depending on the helper, one without its source may sit on a stale value, go unavailable, or never have set up at all. None of those come with a warning.
