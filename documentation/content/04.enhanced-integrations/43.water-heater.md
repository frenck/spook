The water heater :term[integration] is how :term[Home Assistant] controls boilers, heat pump water heaters and other things that keep water hot.

Home Assistant can set a water heater to a temperature, but not turn it up a step. Spook adds the step, the same way it does for [thermostats](/climate).

## Actions

Spook adds two actions for water heaters. You find them wherever you add an action, marked with a 👻.

Both work the same way:

- The step is the one you give, in Home Assistant's unit, or the water heater's own step, or half a degree (one degree in Fahrenheit) when it has none, the same fallback the Home Assistant interface uses.
- The setpoint stays within the minimum and maximum the water heater reports. A step that would cross one stops at it.
- Everything is worked out in the water heater's own unit, so one in Fahrenheit is stepped in whole Fahrenheit degrees and meets its own limits exactly.
- A water heater that is off gets its setpoint moved and stays off.

### Increase temperature

Turns up a water heater's setpoint a step, within the limits it reports.

"A bit warmer" normally means reading the setpoint, adding to it, and keeping it under the maximum yourself. This does all of that. Put it on a button in the bathroom: one press for a slightly hotter shower, without anybody opening the app.

**How to use it**

1. Open the automation or script that should do this, and select **Add action**.
2. Search for **increase temperature**, and select **Water heater: Increase temperature 👻**.
3. Under **Targets**, pick the water heaters.
4. Optionally, set **Step** to how many degrees it should go up. Leave it empty to use the water heater's own step.
5. Select **Save**.

Just want to see it work? [Try this action](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/developer_call_service/?service=water_heater.increase_temperature) opens it in your Home Assistant, ready to run once.

### Decrease temperature

Turns down a water heater's setpoint a step, within the limits it reports.

The other way round from [Increase temperature](#increase-temperature): a bit cooler, never below the minimum. Turn the boiler down a step when everybody has left for the day, without having to know where it was set.

**How to use it**

1. Open the automation or script that should do this, and select **Add action**.
2. Search for **decrease temperature**, and select **Water heater: Decrease temperature 👻**.
3. Under **Targets**, pick the water heaters.
4. Optionally, set **Step** to how many degrees it should go down. Leave it empty to use the water heater's own step.
5. Select **Save**.

Just want to see it work? [Try this action](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/developer_call_service/?service=water_heater.decrease_temperature) opens it in your Home Assistant, ready to run once.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="Known limitations" icon="i-mdi-alert-outline"}

- A water heater without a setpoint, like one that can only be switched on and off, cannot be targeted.
- A water heater that has not reported a setpoint yet is left alone.
- A setpoint already past one of the water heater's own limits, which some integrations report, is left where it is, and not pulled back.

:::
