The Riemann sum integral :term[helper] adds up the readings of a source sensor over time. It is mostly used to work out total energy usage from a power meter: its power readings, and the time between them. Watts in, kilowatt-hours out.

The math behind it is the <wiki:Riemann_sum>: an approximation of an **[integral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integral)** by a [finite sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summation).

## Repairs

Spook looks through your Riemann sum integral helpers for source sensors that no longer exist, and tells you in the :term[repairs dashboard]{to="repairs"}. The repair names the helper and the source that is missing.

This one Spook can fix for you, straight from the repair. New to repairs? Follow [Fix your first repair](/fix-your-first-repair).

### Unknown source entity

A Riemann sum integral helper adds up a source sensor that no longer exists, usually because the sensor was renamed, replaced or removed. Without its source, the helper cannot work.

**How to fix it:** Open the repair. Choose **Let me fix it myself**, open the helper from the Helpers page, and pick a source sensor that exists. Or, if you no longer need the helper, choose **Remove this helper**. **Keep it, stop telling me** leaves it as it is. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

Spook looks at the source sensor :term[entity]{to="entity"} of every Riemann sum integral helper. When that sensor is gone, it raises a :term[repair]{to="repairs"} that names the helper.

This is the same check Spook runs for every helper that is built on a source sensor, described under [Unknown helper sources](/homeassistant#unknown-helper-sources). It reads the settings of the helper itself, so it also finds a helper whose entity failed to set up. Helpers set up in :term[YAML] are not checked.

Before removing a helper, Spook looks again. If its sources changed in the meantime, the helper is left alone, and the repair is updated.
