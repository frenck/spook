The climate :term[integration] is how :term[Home Assistant] controls thermostats, heat pumps and air conditioners.

Home Assistant can set a thermostat to a temperature, but not turn it up a step. A "one degree warmer" button has to read the setpoint (the temperature the thermostat aims for), add to it, and send it back. For a thermostat with two setpoints, nobody gets that template right on the first try. Spook adds the step.

## Actions

Spook adds two actions for thermostats. You find them wherever you add an action, marked with a 👻.

### Increase temperature

Turns up a thermostat's setpoint a step, within the limits it reports.

Think of a "one degree warmer" button on a dashboard, or an automation that makes the bathroom a bit warmer on a cold morning. Without this, each of those is a template that reads the setpoint, adds to it and sends it back. For a thermostat that heats and cools with two setpoints, that template is where people give up. This action does the step for you.

**How to use it**

1. Open the automation or script that should do this, and select **Add action**.
2. Search for **increase temperature**, and select **Climate: Increase temperature 👻**.
3. Under **Targets**, choose the thermostats.
4. Leave **Step** empty to use the thermostat's own step, or fill in how many degrees, like `1`.
5. Select **Save**.

Just want to see it work? [Try this action](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/developer_call_service/?service=climate.increase_temperature) opens it in your Home Assistant, ready to run once.

Both actions, increase and decrease, work the same way:

- The step is the one you give, or the thermostat's own step, or half a degree (one degree in Fahrenheit) when it has none, the same fallback the Home Assistant interface uses.
- A thermostat with one setpoint has it moved. A thermostat that heats below one setpoint and cools above another has both moved, so the band between them keeps its width. In heat and cool mode, the band is what moves, also on a thermostat that reports a single setpoint as well.
- The setpoint stays within the minimum and maximum the thermostat reports. A step that would cross one stops at it, and a band stops whole when either side reaches a limit.
- A thermostat that is off gets its setpoint moved and stays off.

:::

:::accordion-item{label="Example actions in YAML" icon="i-mdi-code-braces"}

Turn the living room up one degree:

```yaml
action: climate.increase_temperature
target:
  entity_id: climate.living_room
data:
  step: 1
```

:::

:::accordion-item{label="Known limitations" icon="i-mdi-alert-outline"}

- A thermostat without a setpoint, like one that only does fan speeds, cannot be targeted.
- A thermostat in a mode without a setpoint at that moment, or that has not reported one yet, is left alone.
- A setpoint already past one of the thermostat's own limits, which some integrations report, is left where it is and not pulled back. So turning it up never makes it colder or jumps it further than the step.

:::
::

### Decrease temperature

Turns down a thermostat's setpoint a step, within the limits it reports.

The other way round from [Increase temperature](#increase-temperature): a "bit cooler" button, or an automation that turns the bedroom down a step at night. Same rules and limits, the other direction.

**How to use it**

1. Open the automation or script that should do this, and select **Add action**.
2. Search for **decrease temperature**, and select **Climate: Decrease temperature 👻**.
3. Under **Targets**, choose the thermostats.
4. Leave **Step** empty to use the thermostat's own step, or fill in how many degrees, like `1`.
5. Select **Save**.

Just want to see it work? [Try this action](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/developer_call_service/?service=climate.decrease_temperature) opens it in your Home Assistant, ready to run once.
