A scene in :term[Home Assistant] is a snapshot of how a group of :term[entities]{to="entity"} should be. Lights in the living room at a warm dim glow, the media player at a quiet volume. Activate the scene, and everything in it goes back to that.

A scene keeps the entity IDs it was made with. Rename or replace a light, and the scene still points at the old one. Activating the scene cannot set a light that is not there, and you get no error. Spook raises a :term[repair]{to="repairs"} when a scene uses an entity that no longer exists.

## Repairs

Spook looks through your scenes for entities that are gone, and tells you in the :term[repairs dashboard]{to="repairs"}. Each repair names the scene and the entities it cannot find.

New to repairs? Follow [Fix your first repair](/fix-your-first-repair).

### Unknown referenced entities

A scene uses an entity that no longer exists, usually because it was renamed, replaced or removed. Activating the scene can no longer set it.

::screenshot{name="repair-scene-unknown-entities" alt="A repair raised by Spook for a scene."}
Spook found an issue with a scene that is using non-existing entities.

::

**How to fix it:** Open the scene from the repair. Remove the missing entity from it, or add the entity that replaced it, and select **Save**. The repair disappears by itself once it is fixed.

::accordion
:::accordion-item{label="How Spook checks this" icon="i-mdi-magnify"}

Spook looks at every :term[entity]{to="entity"} your scenes use. When an :term[entity ID] does not exist, it raises a repair that names the scene and the missing entity ID.

Spook looks again when scenes are reloaded, which saving one in the editor does, when an integration loads, and when the entity registry changes.

A scene set up in YAML without a unique ID has no page in the editor. For that one, the repair links to the list of scenes, and you fix it in your YAML.

:::
::
