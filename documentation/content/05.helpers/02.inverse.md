The inverse :term[helper] turns a switch, binary sensor, cover or valve upside down. On becomes off, and off becomes on. Open becomes closed. The world is upside down!

You want this when a device works the other way round from what you expect. A door sensor that says "open" when the door is shut, a relay wired the opposite way, or a manufacturer that decided to use the opposite logic (yeah... they exist... 🤦‍♂️). Blinds with the motor mounted on the other side, a projector screen that opens by coming down: same story.

It inverts the state of the source :term[entity], and does all :term[actions]{to="performing actions"} in reverse too. Turn the inverted switch on in an automation, and the source turns off.

## Inverting the behavior of an entity

You set it up in the Home Assistant interface, no YAML involved. Add one directly to your own instance by selecting the :term[My Home Assistant] button below:

[![Open your Home Assistant instance and start setting up a new integration.](https://my.home-assistant.io/badges/config_flow_start.svg)](https://my.home-assistant.io/redirect/config_flow_start/?domain=spook_inverse)

Or add one by hand:

1. Go to **Settings** > **Devices & services**, and select the **Helpers** tab.
2. Select **+ Create helper**.
3. Search for **inverse**, and select **Inverse 👻**.

::screenshot{name="helpers-ghost-search" alt="Add helper dialog, which lists the inverse helper."}

::

4. Pick what you want to make: **Invert a binary sensor**, **Invert a cover**, **Invert a switch** or **Invert a valve**.

::screenshot{name="helper-inverse-menu" alt="Inverse helper dialog, which allows you to select the type of entity to invert."}

::

5. Give the new entity a **Name**, and pick the entity to invert under **Source entity**.
6. Turn on **Hide source entity** if you only want to see the inverted one. Hidden is not gone: the source keeps working, and still shows in the entities list.
7. Making a cover? Choose what to turn around with **Invert opening, closing and position** and **Invert tilt**. More on that in [Inverting a cover](#inverting-a-cover).

::screenshot{name="helper-inverse-switch" alt="Inverse helper dialog, configuring the new inverted entity."}

::

8. Select **Submit**. Done! 🎉

Changed your mind later? Open the helper from the **Helpers** tab, and change the source or the options there. No need to start over.

## What can be inverted

- An inverted **switch** can be made of a switch, an on/off toggle helper (`input_boolean`), or a light. Turning the inverted switch on turns the source off, with the source's own actions: a light is turned off as a light.
- An inverted **binary sensor** can read a binary sensor, an on/off toggle helper, or a light.
- An inverted **cover** is made of a cover, and an inverted **valve** of a valve, as explained below.

A light inverted as a switch is only on or off: its brightness, colors and effects stay with the light itself.

## Inverting a cover

A cover can be backwards in two ways, and the inverse helper lets you turn around either one, or both:

- **Invert opening, closing and position** (on by default): opening becomes closing and the other way around, and a position becomes its opposite. A cover that is 30% open is shown as 70% open, and telling the inverse to go to 70% sends the source to 30%.
- **Invert tilt** (off by default): the same for the tilt of the slats. Handy for blinds that open and close fine, but tilt the wrong way.

The position decides whether the inverted cover is closed, not the state of the source. A cover counts as closed only at exactly 0%, and as open at anything above that. So the inverted cover is closed only when the source is all the way open. A cover that cannot report its position only knows open or closed, and has just that turned around.

## Inverting a valve

A valve works the same way as a cover, without the tilt: opening becomes closing, a position becomes its opposite, and the inverted valve is closed only when the source is all the way open. There are no extra options, as a valve only moves one way.
